The positions list sits at the bottom of the **Trading** page. It reads directly from
Hyperliquid and your vault, not from Kimia's own records. What you see is what the
exchange believes, which is the number that matters.

## Two tabs

| Tab | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Positions** | Each open pair as a single row, with both sides together |
| **Spot Holdings** | Spot you hold on its own, on Hyperliquid or in your vault, including leftovers |

## Reading a row

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pair | The asset. The arrow beside it opens a share card |
| Size | The short's size, with the entry price underneath |
| Spot | The side you own outright |
| Short | The perp that cancels the price exposure and collects funding |
| Net | Your remaining exposure to price. Close to zero when the hedge is healthy |
| Funding | The current funding rate, and what it pays per day |
| Earned | Funding collected so far |
| uPnL | Unrealized profit and loss for the whole pair |

A countdown shows when the next funding payment lands.

One side will usually show a gain while the other shows a loss. That is the hedge
working.

## What to actually watch

**The funding rate on your asset.** It changes every hour. If it has turned negative
and stayed there, you are now paying to hold the position rather than being paid.

**The margin on your short.** The short uses isolated margin. If the price has risen
a long way since you opened, that margin has shrunk. Spare balance elsewhere on
Hyperliquid does not help unless you add it to the position yourself, at the
exchange.

**Your vault authorization**, for Kimia spot. It lasts 30 days and later buys do not
always renew it. If it lapses, Kimia will not close the pair. Renew it on the Vault
tab. See [The Kimia vault](/using-kimia/kimia-vault#the-authorization).

**How long you have held it.** Opening and closing cost money. A position needs to
run long enough for the funding collected to exceed what you paid to get in and out.

## What not to worry about

The spot and short sizes drifting slightly apart. Small mismatches are normal.

Unrealized profit and loss on either side on its own.

Short-term swings in the funding rate. One negative hour is noise.

## What Kimia does for you, and what it does not

**Kimia does:** sell your spot automatically if your short is liquidated, so you are
not left holding it unhedged. See [Risks](/safety/risks#liquidation-on-the-short-side).

**Kimia does not:** rebalance a position, top up its margin, prevent liquidation, or
close it when funding turns negative. Those calls are yours.

## When to consider closing

There is no schedule and no maturity date. Reasonable moments to close:

- Funding has been negative for a sustained period on your asset
- The margin on your short has shrunk and you do not want to add more
- You need the money for something else
- A different asset is paying meaningfully more and you have held long enough to
  cover the cost of switching
