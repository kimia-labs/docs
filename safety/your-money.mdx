## Kimia does not hold your funds

Your money sits in your own master wallets, and in your own accounts at Hyperliquid
and Pacifica that belong to those wallets. Kimia has no account holding customer
deposits and cannot move your funds to itself or to anyone else.

You can withdraw at any time, through the exchange or on-chain, without Kimia's
involvement or permission.

## The wallets

**The wallet you sign in with** is yours, from before Kimia. It proves who you are
and pays for in-app deposits. It never holds your exchange accounts.

**Your master wallets**, one on Ethereum and one on Solana, are created for you when
you sign in, by Kimia's sign-in provider, Privy. They hold your exchange accounts and
your money. They are yours: you can take their keys out at any time with
**Export key** in the account menu.

**The agent** is an address Kimia creates and runs. Once bound, it may place orders
on your Hyperliquid and Pacifica accounts. It holds no money of its own.

## What your master wallets sign

| Signature | How often | What it allows |
|---|---|---|
| Bind the agent | Once per exchange | The agent may trade on that exchange account |
| Approve Kimia's fee | Once per exchange | The exchange may charge Kimia's fee, up to 0.1 percent, on orders Kimia places |
| Jupiter swap | Every Jupiter buy and sell | That one swap, shown in the prompt |

Deposits are signed by the wallet you sign in with, not the master wallets.

## What the agent can and cannot do

| Can | Cannot |
|---|---|
| Open positions | Withdraw funds |
| Close positions | Send money to another address |
| Cancel orders | Approve further permissions |
| Change leverage and margin mode | Sign Jupiter swaps |
| Move USDC between your own Hyperliquid spot and perp balances | Access your master wallets |

The worst case if the agent were misused is unwanted trading on your account, which
could lose money. It is not the same as your funds being taken. The exchanges
enforce the line between trading and withdrawing, not Kimia.

## Jupiter holdings

When Jupiter is the spot venue, the asset you buy sits in your Solana master wallet
as an ordinary token, not at an exchange. The agent cannot touch it. Every sale needs
your signature.

## Getting your keys

Your master wallets live with Privy until you export them. To use them anywhere
outside Kimia, including at an exchange's own website:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the account menu">
    Top right. Go to the **Wallets** tab.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Press Export key on the wallet you need">
    **Ethereum master** for Hyperliquid, **Solana master** for Pacifica and Jupiter.
    Privy shows the key in its own secure window.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Import it into a wallet you control">
    MetaMask for the Ethereum master, Phantom or Solflare for the Solana master. Keep
    the key private. Anyone with it controls the wallet.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Do this before you need it. It is your route to your money if Kimia is ever
unavailable.

## Revoking access

You can revoke the agent at any time at the exchange, where approved agents are
listed. Connect with your exported master wallet to do it. Revoking takes effect
immediately and does not need Kimia to cooperate.

After revoking:

- Open positions stay open. Revoking does not close anything
- Kimia can no longer place or close orders for you on that exchange
- You manage or close remaining positions at the exchange directly

Revoke if you stop using Kimia, or at any point you are uncomfortable.

## If something looks wrong

Trades you did not start, or a position you do not recognise, warrant acting first
and asking afterwards.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Export your master wallet keys">
    See above. You need them for the next steps.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Revoke the agent at the exchange">
    This stops any further orders immediately.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Close open positions at the exchange">
    You do not need Kimia to do this.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Withdraw if you want to">
    Your master wallets keep full control throughout.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Two exchanges, two separate accounts

Binding Hyperliquid does not bind Pacifica, and revoking one does not revoke the
other. Each is separate, with separate funds and a separate permission.

If you decide to stop using Kimia entirely, revoke on both.
