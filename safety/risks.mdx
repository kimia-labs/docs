Delta-neutral does not mean risk-free. It means one particular risk, the price going
up or down, has largely been removed. Several others have not.

Read this before depositing.

## Funding can turn negative

The yield is a fee paid by leveraged buyers. When sentiment flips and more people are
betting the price will fall, the direction reverses and the sellers start paying the
buyers.

Your position then costs you money every hour instead of paying you.

This is not rare. It happens during sharp downturns, which is also when you are least
likely to want to close.

**What you can do.** Watch the rate on your asset. Close if it stays negative. Kimia
does not close for you.

## Liquidation on the short side

The short position is leveraged, which means it is backed by a margin buffer rather
than the full amount. Kimia uses isolated margin, so that buffer is only the margin
posted for that position. The rest of your balance does not back it.

If the price rises far enough, that buffer runs out and the exchange closes the
position for you. This is liquidation. You lose the buffer, and worse, your hedge is
gone while you still hold the asset. You are now fully exposed to the price.

Higher leverage makes this happen after a smaller move.

**What you can do.** Use low leverage. Watch the margin after large price moves, and
add to it at the exchange if needed. Kimia does not top it up for you.

## Forced closure even when you are safe

This one surprises people, and it is worth understanding.

When an exchange cannot find a buyer for a liquidated position, it closes somebody
else's profitable position instead to balance the books. This is called auto
deleveraging.

Exchanges pick which positions to close by ranking them, and the ranking favours
positions that are profitable and leveraged. A profitable leveraged short is exactly
what the Kimia strategy holds.

Being hedged does not protect you. Only the perp side gets closed, leaving you holding
the asset with no hedge, without having done anything wrong and without warning.

**What you can do.** Very little, directly. Lower leverage reduces your ranking. It is
a real risk of the strategy and it is not modelled in any published return figure,
including ours.

## The hedge is approximate

The two sides are sized from prices captured moments apart, and each exchange rounds
order sizes differently. When the two sides live on different venues, their prices
can drift apart independently. That drift tends to be largest exactly when funding is
most attractive.

The result is that your exposure is close to zero, not zero. Over a long hold, that
gap accumulates.

## Exchange and venue risk

Your funds sit at Hyperliquid, at Pacifica, or in your Solana master wallet as tokens
bought through Jupiter. If an exchange is hacked, halts withdrawals, or fails, your
deposit there is at risk regardless of anything Kimia does.

Pacifica is a newer venue than Hyperliquid. Size accordingly.

Deposits into Hyperliquid pass through Across, a cross-chain bridge. Bridges carry
their own risk while funds are in transit.

## Wallet risk

Your master wallets are created and held by Privy until you export their keys. If you
lose access to your sign-in wallet, you may lose access to them too. Export your keys
and keep them safe. See [Your money](/safety/your-money#getting-your-keys).

Once exported, anyone who obtains those keys controls your funds.

## Software risk

Kimia is unaudited software in private beta. It places real orders with real money.

Its safety checks refuse unsafe orders rather than proceeding, which is the right
default, but no amount of checking makes early software equivalent to mature software.

The agent's permission is limited to trading and cannot withdraw funds, which caps
the worst case. It does not cap the risk of a badly placed or badly timed order.

## Fees exceeding yield

Covered in [What it costs](/using-kimia/what-it-costs), and it deserves listing here
because it is the most common way people actually lose money on this strategy.

Open and close frequently enough and the fees will exceed everything the funding rate
paid you, no matter how good the rate was.

## What is not a risk

Worth stating clearly, because these worry people unnecessarily.

Kimia cannot withdraw your funds. The agent's limits are enforced by the exchange.

A large unrealized loss on one side of your position, when matched by a gain on the
other, is the hedge working correctly.

Kimia being offline does not affect an open position. It stays open at the exchange,
and you can close it there directly using your exported master wallet key.

<Warning>
Start small. Hold one position through a full cycle, including a period of negative
funding, before committing an amount that would hurt to lose.
</Warning>
