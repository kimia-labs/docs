title: "What it costs"
description: "The fees between the headline rate and what you keep."
---

The Carry APR you see quoted is gross. Several things come out of it before the money
is yours.

## Kimia's fee

Kimia charges **0.05 percent of the short side's size, when it opens and again when
it closes**. That is 0.10 percent over a round trip. There is no Kimia fee on the spot
side.

| Short size | Fee to open | Fee to close | Round trip |
|---|---|---|---|
| 500 USD | 0.25 USD | 0.25 USD | 0.50 USD |
| 5,000 USD | 2.50 USD | 2.50 USD | 5.00 USD |

Kimia does not take this from your account itself. The exchange collects it with the
order and passes it on, as what exchanges call a builder fee. That is why your master
wallet approves it once on each exchange. The approval allows up to 0.1 percent, so
Kimia could raise the fee to that ceiling without asking again. If it does, it will be
stated here first.

Part of Kimia's fee may go to whoever invited you. It never adds to what you pay. See
[Referrals](/using-kimia/referrals).

## Exchange costs

**Trading fees.** Every exchange charges a fee on every order. You pay it four times
over the life of a position: buying and shorting on the way in, and closing both on
the way out.

**Jupiter swap fee.** When Jupiter is the spot venue, each swap carries a small fee,
shown in the signing prompt.

**The spread.** Buying pushes the price up slightly and selling pushes it down. On
liquid assets this is negligible. On thin ones it is not, and it grows with your
order size. The ticket warns you when spot and perp are more than 2 percent apart.

**Network fees.** Jupiter swaps are Solana transactions and cost a small amount of
SOL each. Hyperliquid and Pacifica orders do not.

**Dust.** The small remainder left behind when you close. See
[Closing a position](/using-kimia/closing).

## Getting money in and out

**Bridging.** The in-app deposit and the Bridge tab use Across, which takes roughly
1.26 USD per transfer whatever the size. See [Deposit and bridge](/getting-started/add-funds).

**Withdrawing.** Whatever the exchange or network charges. Kimia adds nothing.

## Why holding period matters more than rate

Almost all of these costs are paid when you open and close. The yield accrues every
hour. So the same position can be profitable or unprofitable depending only on how
long you keep it.

A rate that looks excellent will not cover the round trip if you close after two
days. A modest rate held for two months usually will.

This is the single most common way to lose money on a strategy that is otherwise
working: opening and closing too often.

## Working out whether it is worth it

Before opening, ask roughly: how many days of funding does it take to pay back the
cost of getting in and out. If the answer is longer than you intend to hold, do not
open the position.

A rough example. A 1,000 USD position at 2x puts about 667 USD on each side. The
round trip costs about 0.67 USD in Kimia fees, plus four exchange fees and the
spread. At a Carry APR of 10 percent, the short earns about 0.18 USD a day. The
Kimia fee alone takes about four days to earn back, before exchange fees.

<Note>
Carry APR is the next hour's expected rate multiplied by 8,760 hours. It assumes that
rate holds for a year. Funding rates rarely hold for a month. Treat it as a
description of the present, not a projection.
</Note>
