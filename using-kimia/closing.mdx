---
title: "Closing a position"
description: "Getting your money back out."
---

Close from your positions list. Kimia unwinds both sides for you.

## What happens

The short side closes first, then the asset is sold.

That order is deliberate. If the second step fails, you are left holding the asset,
which you can sell yourself at any time. The reverse order could leave you holding
only a short position, which is exposed to the price rising.

On Hyperliquid, any margin left sitting behind the closed short is swept back
automatically once the position is gone.

## Partial closes

You can close one side on its own if you need to. Be clear about what that means:
closing one side removes the hedge. What remains is a plain directional bet, either
on the price rising or on it falling.

If you do this by accident, close the other side too.

## The dust that gets left behind

You will usually be left with a very small amount of the asset, worth a fraction of a
dollar. It is not a bug and it is not recoverable.

The reason is that exchanges charge spot fees in the asset you receive, and they only
accept order sizes in fixed increments. Buy a round number, pay the fee in the asset,
and the amount you now hold is no longer a round number. Selling all of it is
impossible, so a remainder stays behind.

Kimia refuses to open positions on assets where this remainder would be large. The
limit is one dollar. Assets that cannot be exited cleanly within that are not offered.

## How long it takes

Both sides close within seconds under normal conditions. The funds return to your
exchange account, not to Kimia.

From there you withdraw through the exchange directly, the same way you deposited.

## If a close fails partway

Check your positions list. Whatever is still open is still open, and you can close it
again.

If one side closed and the other did not, close the remaining side promptly. You are
unhedged until you do, which means you are exposed to the price.

If closing keeps failing, see [Troubleshooting](/safety/troubleshooting). You can also
close positions directly through the exchange's own interface, which does not depend
on Kimia working.
