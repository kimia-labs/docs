---
title: "Glossary"
description: "Terms you will meet, in plain language."
---

**Agent**
An address Kimia creates and you approve, allowed to place orders on your exchange
account but not to withdraw funds.

**Auto deleveraging**
When an exchange closes somebody's profitable position to cover a loss it cannot
otherwise absorb. It can affect you even when your position is perfectly safe. See
[Risks](/safety/risks).

**Basis**
The gap between the price of an asset and the price of its perpetual future. When it
moves, a hedge that was exact becomes slightly inexact.

**Delta**
Exposure to price. A position with high delta gains and loses as the price moves.

**Delta-neutral**
Holding two positions that offset each other, so price movement affects you very
little. The strategy Kimia runs.

**Dust**
A very small leftover amount of an asset after closing, too small to sell. Usually
worth a fraction of a dollar.

**Funding rate**
A fee paid every hour between the two sides of a perpetual future, keeping its price
tethered to the real one. The source of your yield.

**Hedge**
A position taken to cancel out the risk of another one.

**Leverage**
Controlling a larger position than the money you put up. Increases both the share of
your capital that earns and the chance of liquidation.

**Liquidation**
The exchange closing your leveraged position because its margin buffer ran out. You
lose the buffer.

**Long**
A position that gains when the price rises.

**Margin**
Money set aside to back a leveraged position. The buffer that absorbs adverse price
moves.

**Master wallet**
Your main address. Holds the exchange account and the money. Signs once, during
setup.

**Notional**
The full value of a position, as opposed to the money you posted to open it. A 500
USD position at 5x leverage has a notional of 500 USD and used 100 USD of margin.

**Perpetual future, or perp**
A contract for betting on an asset's price with leverage that never expires. Kept in
line with the real price by the funding rate.

**Short**
A position that gains when the price falls. The side that usually collects funding.

**Slippage**
The difference between the price you expected and the price you got, caused by your
own order moving the market.

**Spot**
Buying the asset itself, outright, with no leverage and no expiry.

**Stale data**
Market data old enough that Kimia will not price an order against it. Causes a refusal
rather than a risky order.
