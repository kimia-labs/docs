Delta-neutral does not mean risk-free. It means one particular risk, the price going
up or down, has largely been removed. Several others have not.

Read this before depositing.

## Funding can turn negative

The yield is a fee paid by leveraged buyers. When sentiment flips and more people are
betting the price will fall, the direction reverses and the sellers start paying the
buyers.

Your position then costs you money every hour instead of paying you.

This is not rare. It happens during sharp downturns, which is also when you are least
likely to want to close.

**What you can do.** Watch the rate on your asset. Close if it stays negative. Kimia
does not close for you.

## Liquidation on the short side

The short is leveraged, which means it is backed by a margin buffer rather than the
full amount. Kimia uses isolated margin, so that buffer is only the margin posted for
that position.

If the price rises far enough, that buffer runs out and Hyperliquid closes the short.
This is liquidation. You lose the margin.

Kimia then sells your spot automatically, usually within a couple of minutes, so you
are not left holding it unhedged. That sale accepts up to 5 percent slippage, and the
price may have moved in the meantime. For Kimia spot whose authorization has lapsed,
or while the vault is paused, Kimia returns the tokens to your Solana master unsold
instead. Kimia only acts on liquidations it notices within an hour.

Higher leverage makes liquidation happen after a smaller move.

**What you can do.** Use low leverage. Watch the margin after large price moves, and
add to it at Hyperliquid if needed. Kimia does not top it up for you.

## Forced closure even when you are safe

When an exchange cannot find a buyer for a liquidated position, it closes somebody
else's profitable position instead to balance the books. This is called auto
deleveraging.

Exchanges pick which positions to close by ranking them, and the ranking favours
positions that are profitable and leveraged. A profitable leveraged short is exactly
what this strategy holds.

Only the short gets closed, leaving you holding spot with no hedge, without warning.
Kimia does not treat this as a liquidation and does not sell the spot for you.

**What you can do.** Very little, directly. Lower leverage reduces your ranking. Check
your positions after sharp moves. It is a real risk of the strategy and it is not
modelled in any published return figure, including ours.

## The hedge is approximate

Kimia sizes the short to the spot that actually arrived, but each exchange rounds
order sizes differently, and when spot is in the Kimia vault, its price on Solana can
drift apart from Hyperliquid's. That drift tends to be largest exactly when funding is
most attractive.

Your exposure is close to zero, not zero.

## Kimia vault risk

Kimia spot sits in a vault program on Solana. That adds risks a plain exchange account
does not have:

- **Smart contract risk.** The program is unaudited. A bug could lose funds
- **Manager key risk.** Kimia's manager key can sell vault tokens without your
  signature or authorization, even while the vault is paused, and nothing on-chain
  sets a minimum price. If that key were compromised,
  tokens could be sold at a bad price. Proceeds could only go back to their owners
- **Upgrade risk.** Kimia can upgrade the program. Moving that power to a multisig
  with a time delay is planned, not done
- **Authorization expiry.** Authorizations last 30 days and are not always renewed by
  later buys. If yours lapses, Kimia will not close a pair holding that token until you
  reauthorize, which needs some USDC deposited in the vault
- **Shared accounts.** Tokens of the same kind sit in one account owned by the program,
  with each owner's share recorded separately. If USDC's issuer froze that account,
  every user's USDC in it would be stuck

## Exchange and bridge risk

Your funds sit at Hyperliquid, in your Solana master, or in the Kimia vault. If
Hyperliquid is hacked, halts withdrawals, or fails, your deposit there is at risk
regardless of anything Kimia does.

Deposits and withdrawals between Solana and Hyperliquid pass through Circle's CCTP
bridge. If a transfer does not arrive, it needs Circle or Kimia support to trace.

## Wallet risk

Your master wallets are held by Privy until you export their keys. If you lose access
to your sign-in wallet, you may lose access to them too. Export your keys and keep
them safe. See [Your money](/safety/your-money#getting-your-keys).

Once exported, anyone who obtains those keys controls your funds.

## Software risk

Kimia is unaudited software in private beta. It places real orders with real money.

Its safety checks refuse unsafe orders rather than proceeding, which is the right
default, but no amount of checking makes early software equivalent to mature software.

## Fees exceeding yield

Covered in [What it costs](/using-kimia/what-it-costs), and it deserves listing here
because it is the most common way people actually lose money on this strategy.

Open and close frequently enough and the fees will exceed everything the funding rate
paid you, no matter how good the rate was.

## What is not a risk

Worth stating clearly, because these worry people unnecessarily.

Kimia cannot send your funds to anyone but you. Hyperliquid enforces that for your
exchange account, and the vault program enforces it for your vault.

A large unrealized loss on one side of your position, when matched by a gain on the
other, is the hedge working correctly.

Kimia being offline does not affect an open position. It stays open, and you can close
it at Hyperliquid and withdraw from your vault using your exported keys.

<Warning>
Start small. Hold one position through a full cycle, including a period of negative
funding, before committing an amount that would hurt to lose.
</Warning>
