## An invite code

Kimia is in private beta. You need an invite code from an existing user to get in.
Codes read `KIM` followed by eight letters or digits. See
[Invite codes](/getting-started/invite-code).

## A crypto wallet

Kimia signs you in with a wallet you already have. Phantom, MetaMask and Solflare all
work.

This wallet proves who you are. It is not the wallet that trades. When you sign in,
Kimia creates two new wallets for you, one on Ethereum and one on Solana, and those
hold the money the exchanges see. [Create your account](/getting-started/create-your-account)
explains why.

## USDC on Solana, and a little SOL

The simplest way in is USDC on Solana, sitting in the wallet you sign in with. The
in-app deposit takes it from there and splits it between the exchanges for you. See
[Deposit and bridge](/getting-started/add-funds).

Keep about 0.01 SOL in the same wallet. The deposit creates a small on-chain account
that has to be paid for in SOL.

You can also send funds straight to each exchange wallet instead:

| Exchange | Runs on | Send |
|---|---|---|
| Hyperliquid | Its own chain, HyperCore | USDC on HyperCore to your Ethereum master |
| Pacifica | Solana | USDC on Solana to your Solana master |
| Jupiter (spot only) | Solana | Spends from your Solana master directly |

Money on one exchange cannot open a position on another. Once it is there, nothing
moves it between them automatically.

## A sensible starting amount

Each side of a position must be worth at least 10 USD. At 1x leverage that is 20 USD
in total, and at 2x it is 15 USD.

That is the technical minimum, not a recommendation. The deposit bridge takes a flat
fee of around 1.26 USD, and each trade pays exchange fees. At very small sizes those
costs eat most of what you earn. A few hundred dollars is a more realistic place to
start seeing the yield clearly.

## About twenty minutes

Most of it is waiting for deposits to confirm. The Kimia side takes a couple of
minutes.

<Note>
Expect a few signing prompts during setup: one to approve trading on each exchange,
one to approve Kimia's fee on each exchange, and two for the deposit. None of them
grant a withdrawal permission. See [Your money](/safety/your-money) for exactly what
each one allows.
</Note>

## What you do not need

You do not need to understand perpetual futures in detail. You do not need to watch
charts or time an entry. You do not need to keep a browser tab open, since positions
stay open at the exchange whether or not you are looking at them.
