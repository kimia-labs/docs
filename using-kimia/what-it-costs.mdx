The Carry APR you see quoted is gross. Several things come out of it before the money
is yours.

## Kimia's fee

Kimia charges **0.05 percent of the short's size, when it opens and again when it
closes**. That is 0.10 percent over a round trip. There is no Kimia fee on the spot
side, whether on Hyperliquid or in the Kimia vault.

| Short size | Fee to open | Fee to close | Round trip |
|---|---|---|---|
| 500 USD | 0.25 USD | 0.25 USD | 0.50 USD |
| 5,000 USD | 2.50 USD | 2.50 USD | 5.00 USD |

Kimia does not take this from your account itself. Hyperliquid collects it with the
order and passes it on, as what exchanges call a builder fee. That needs your Ethereum
master to approve it once. The approval step is not in the app yet. When it arrives,
you will be asked once before your next trade. The approval allows up to 0.1 percent,
so Kimia could raise the fee to that ceiling without asking again. If it does, it will
be stated here first.

Part of Kimia's fee may go to whoever invited you. It never adds to what you pay. See
[Referrals](/using-kimia/referrals).

## Exchange costs

**Hyperliquid trading fees.** A fee on every order. With Hyperliquid spot you pay it
four times over a position's life: buying and shorting on the way in, closing both on
the way out.

**Kimia vault swaps.** Buying and selling Kimia spot goes through Jupiter, and each
swap pays the route's own cost. The vault only routes through exchanges it can call
safely, which can cost a few hundredths of a percent more than the best route.

**The spread.** Buying pushes the price up slightly and selling pushes it down. On
liquid assets this is negligible. On thin ones it is not, and it grows with your
order size. The ticket warns you when spot and perp are more than 2 percent apart.

**Dust.** The small remainder left behind when you close. See
[Closing a position](/using-kimia/closing).

## Network fees and rent

| Cost | Paid by |
|---|---|
| Solana fees for Kimia vault buys and sells | Kimia |
| One-time rent for a token's vault records, about 0.01 SOL | You, from your Solana master |
| Vault tab steps: deposit, withdraw, revoke, authorize | You, a small SOL fee each |
| Hyperliquid orders | No network fee |

## Getting money in and out

| | Cost |
|---|---|
| Deposit or bridge to HyperCore | About 0.25 USD, plus 1 USD once on your first HyperCore deposit |
| Withdraw or bridge from HyperCore | Circle's fee, taken on arrival, plus 0.50 USD held back for Hyperliquid's gas |
| Withdraw from your Solana master | A small SOL network fee |

Kimia adds nothing to any of these. See [Deposit and bridge](/getting-started/add-funds)
and [Withdraw](/getting-started/withdraw).

## Why holding period matters more than rate

Almost all of these costs are paid when you open and close. The yield accrues every
hour. So the same position can be profitable or unprofitable depending only on how
long you keep it.

A rate that looks excellent will not cover the round trip if you close after two
days. A modest rate held for two months usually will.

This is the single most common way to lose money on a strategy that is otherwise
working: opening and closing too often.

## Working out whether it is worth it

Before opening, ask roughly: how many days of funding does it take to pay back the
cost of getting in and out. If the answer is longer than you intend to hold, do not
open the position.

A rough example. A 1,000 USD position at 2x puts about 667 USD on each side. The
round trip costs about 0.67 USD in Kimia fees, plus exchange fees and the spread. At
a Carry APR of 10 percent, the short earns about 0.18 USD a day. The Kimia fee alone
takes about four days to earn back.

<Note>
Carry APR is the next hour's expected rate multiplied by 8,760 hours. It assumes that
rate holds for a year. Funding rates rarely hold for a month. Treat it as a
description of the present, not a projection.
</Note>
