The positions list sits at the bottom of the **Trading** page. It reads directly from
the exchanges, not from Kimia's own records. What you see is what the exchange
believes, which is the number that matters.

## Two tabs

| Tab | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Positions** | Each open pair as a single row, with both sides together |
| **Spot Holdings** | Assets you hold on their own, including leftovers from closed pairs |

## Reading a row

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pair | The asset, and which venue holds each side |
| Size | The size of the position |
| Spot | The side you own outright |
| Short | The perp that cancels the price exposure and collects funding |
| Net | Your remaining exposure to price. Close to zero when the hedge is healthy |
| Funding | The current funding rate on the short |
| Earned | Funding collected so far |
| uPnL | Unrealized profit and loss |

A countdown shows when the next funding payment lands.

One side will usually show a gain while the other shows a loss. That is the hedge
working. A large loss on the short paired with a matching gain on the spot side means
the price rose and nothing has gone wrong.

## What to actually watch

**The funding rate on your asset.** It changes every hour. If it has turned negative
and stayed there, you are now paying to hold the position rather than being paid.

**The margin on your short.** The short uses isolated margin: it is backed only by
the margin posted for that position. If the price has risen a long way since you
opened, that margin has shrunk. Spare balance elsewhere on the exchange does not help
unless you add it to the position yourself, at the exchange.

**How long you have held it.** Opening and closing cost money. A position needs to
run long enough for the funding collected to exceed what you paid to get in and out.

## What not to worry about

The spot and short sizes drifting slightly apart. Small mismatches are normal.

Unrealized profit and loss on either side on its own. Only the combined figure means
anything.

Short-term swings in the funding rate. It moves constantly. One negative hour is
noise.

## What Kimia does not do for you

Kimia does not rebalance a position, top up its margin, protect it from liquidation,
or close it when funding turns negative. Those calls are yours.

## When to consider closing

There is no schedule and no maturity date. Reasonable moments to close:

- Funding has been negative for a sustained period on your asset
- The margin on your short has shrunk and you do not want to add more
- You need the money for something else
- A different asset is paying meaningfully more and you have held long enough to
  cover the cost of switching
