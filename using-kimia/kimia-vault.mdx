When you pick **Kimia** as the spot venue, the asset is not bought on an exchange. It
is bought inside the Kimia vault, a program on Solana run by Kimia, where your balance
is recorded against your Solana master wallet. Kimia routes the purchase through Jupiter, a Solana exchange aggregator, and the
tokens stay in the vault until the position closes.

## What the vault holds

Your vault keeps two things, both recorded against your Solana master:

- **Deposited USDC**, waiting to be spent on spot buys
- **Tokens** Kimia has bought for you, one balance per asset

The tokens sit in shared accounts owned by the vault program, with your share
recorded separately. Nobody else's position can spend yours.

## What you sign

| Moment | Prompts |
|---|---|
| A buy on a token you have bought before | One |
| The first buy of a token | One, or two if the one-time setup does not fit in the buy's transaction |
| Closing a pair, or selling Kimia spot | None |
| Deposit, withdraw, revoke, authorize or withdraw a token on the Vault tab | One each |

A buy is a single transaction that, where needed, opens your vault records, moves any
missing USDC in from your Solana master, renews your authorization and swaps. Kimia
pays the network fee for buys and sells. If you take too long to sign, Kimia prepares
a fresh transaction once.

## The authorization

Signing a buy also signs an **authorization** for that token. It lets Kimia trade that
token inside your vault:

- **Buying**, up to the USDC you had deposited when you signed
- **Selling**, without limit, so a position can always be closed

It lasts **30 days**. A buy renews it only when what is left does not cover the buy,
or it is about to expire. So a position held longer than 30 days can lose its
authorization.

Kimia's servers will not trade a token whose authorization has expired or been
revoked. That includes closing: Kimia will not close such a pair until you
**Reauthorize** it. Reauthorizing needs some USDC deposited in the vault. If you have
none, use **Withdraw as** the token instead.

## What Kimia can and cannot do in your vault

Kimia acts through a single key it controls, called the manager key.

| The program lets Kimia | The program stops Kimia from |
|---|---|
| Buy a token, within your authorization and with your signature | Buying without your signature, or beyond your authorization |
| Sell a token you hold, back to USDC, at any time | Sending funds to any address except your own Solana wallet |
| Withdraw your funds to your own Solana wallet | Routing swaps anywhere except through Jupiter |

These limits are enforced by the vault program on-chain. On-chain, selling needs no
authorization at all: the program lets the manager key sell any token back to USDC,
even while the vault is paused, so that positions can always be unwound. The rule that
Kimia only sells under a current authorization is Kimia's own policy, applied by its
servers.

<Warning>
Nothing on-chain sets a minimum price when Kimia sells. If Kimia's manager key were
compromised, an attacker could sell vault tokens at a bad price. They could not send
the proceeds anywhere except back to their owners. See [Risks](/safety/risks#kimia-vault-risk).
</Warning>

## Before your first Kimia trade

Your Solana master needs:

- **At least 0.012 SOL.** Checked before every Kimia trade. The first trade of a token
  also pays one-time rent for its vault records. If you are short, the trade ticket's button reads **Add SOL for fees**. Send SOL to the Solana
  master's address, shown in the account menu
- **USDC**, either in the Solana master or already deposited in the vault. The
  Deposit tab sends it there. See [Deposit and bridge](/getting-started/add-funds)

If your account has no Solana master yet, the ticket offers **Create Solana wallet**.
Kimia needs no agent binding.

## The Vault tab

On the **Wallet** page, the **Vault** tab shows your vault and the authorization for
each token.

| Row or button | What it does |
|---|---|
| Deposited USDC | USDC in the vault, ready for buys |
| Wallet USDC, Wallet SOL | What your Solana master holds outside the vault |
| **Deposit**, **Withdraw** | Move USDC between your Solana master and the vault |
| **Withdraw as USDC** | Sell a token and send the USDC to your Solana master |
| **Withdraw as** a token | Take the token itself out of the vault, unsold |
| **Revoke** | Cancel that token's authorization |
| **Authorize**, **Reauthorize** | Grant or renew it |

Each token shows its authorization status, for example "$40.00 left to buy · until
Nov 27", "Authorization expired Nov 27" or "Not authorized".

Each of these steps is a transaction your Solana master signs and pays a small SOL fee
for.

## When the vault is paused

Kimia can pause the vault. While paused, buys and Vault tab deposits are refused, and
Kimia's servers will not close a pair holding Kimia spot. Your own withdrawals still
work: **Withdraw** and **Withdraw as** a token are never blocked by a pause.

## Closing

Closing a pair needs no signature. Kimia closes the Hyperliquid short, sells the vault
tokens, and sends the USDC from the sale to your Solana master. USDC you had deposited
but not spent stays in the vault until you withdraw it. See
[Closing a position](/using-kimia/closing).

## Costs

There is no Kimia fee on the vault side. You pay:

- The swap's own cost, through Jupiter. Kimia only routes through exchanges the vault
  can safely call, which can cost a few hundredths of a percent more than the best
  route
- One-time rent in SOL for each new token's records, about 0.01 SOL on a first trade
- Small SOL fees for the Vault tab steps
