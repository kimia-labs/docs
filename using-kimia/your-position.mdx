Your positions list reads directly from the exchange, not from Kimia's own records.
What you see is what the exchange believes, which is the number that matters.

## The two entries

Every delta-neutral position shows as two entries.

| Entry | What it is |
|---|---|
| Long | The asset you own outright |
| Short | The perp position that cancels the price exposure and collects funding |

One will usually show a gain while the other shows a loss. That is the hedge working.
Look at them together, not separately. A large loss on the short side paired with a
matching gain on the long side means the price rose and nothing has gone wrong.

## What to actually watch

**The funding rate on your asset.** It changes hourly. If it has turned negative and
stayed there, you are now paying to hold the position rather than being paid.

**Your margin buffer on the short side.** This is the number that decides whether you
get liquidated. If the price has risen a long way since you opened, the buffer will
have shrunk. Adding funds to the exchange account restores room.

**How long you have held it.** Opening and closing cost money. A position needs to
run long enough for the funding collected to exceed what you paid to get in and out.

## What not to worry about

The long and short sizes drifting slightly apart. Small mismatches are normal.

Unrealized profit and loss on either side individually. Only the combined figure
means anything.

Short-term swings in the funding rate. It moves constantly. One negative hour is
noise.

## When to consider closing

There is no schedule and no maturity date. Reasonable moments to close:

- Funding has been negative for a sustained period on your asset
- Your margin buffer has shrunk and you do not want to add more funds
- You need the money for something else
- A different asset is paying meaningfully more and you have held long enough to
  cover the cost of switching

See [Closing a position](/using-kimia/closing).
