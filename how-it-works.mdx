## Where the yield comes from

A perpetual future, or perp, is a way to bet on the price of an asset with borrowed
money. Unlike a normal futures contract it never expires, so exchanges need some way
to keep its price tethered to the real one.

They do it with the funding rate. Every hour, one side pays the other.

When more people are betting the price will rise than fall, which is most of the
time in crypto, the buyers pay the sellers. Being on the selling side earns that
payment, hour after hour, for as long as you hold.

The catch is obvious. Betting the price will fall means losing money when it rises.

## Cancelling out the price

Kimia solves that by taking both sides at once.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Buy the asset outright">
    You own it. If the price doubles, this position doubles.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sell the same asset as a perp">
    This position loses exactly what the first one gains, and gains exactly what it
    loses.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Collect the funding rate">
    The two positions cancel out on price. The funding payment does not cancel. It is
    what you keep.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The industry name for holding two positions that offset each other is
**delta-neutral**. Delta means exposure to price. Neutral means close to none.

## A worked example

Suppose you start with 1,000 USD and the funding rate on an asset is 10 percent
annualized.

| | |
|---|---|
| You buy | 500 USD of the asset |
| You sell short | 500 USD of the same asset as a perp |
| Your price exposure | Close to zero |
| The perp pays you | About 10 percent per year on 500 USD |

That is roughly 50 USD a year, or 5 percent on your 1,000 USD. Half your money is
doing the earning, because the other half is holding the hedge in place.

## Why leverage changes the maths

You can post less money against the short position and borrow the rest. That frees up
more of your capital to sit in the earning position.

| Leverage | Share of your money earning funding |
|---|---|
| 1x | 50 percent |
| 2x | 67 percent |
| 3x | 75 percent |
| 5x | 83 percent |

The gains shrink quickly after about 3x, and the risk does not. Higher leverage means
a smaller price move can trigger a liquidation on the short side, which breaks the
hedge at the worst possible moment. See [Risks](/safety/risks).

## Three venues

Kimia works across three venues.

| Venue | Buy the asset | Sell the perp | Runs on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hyperliquid | Yes | Yes | Its own chain, HyperCore |
| Pacifica | Yes | Yes | Solana |
| Jupiter | Yes | No | Solana |

Sometimes the best combination is buying the asset on one venue and shorting it on
another, because prices and funding rates differ. Kimia marks the venue it
recommends for each side.

This matters for a practical reason: the venues live on different blockchains. The
in-app deposit splits your USDC between Solana and Hyperliquid for you, but once it
is there, nothing moves money between them automatically. See
[Deposit and bridge](/getting-started/add-funds).

## What you are actually being paid for

It helps to be clear about this. You are not being paid for taking no risk. You are
being paid for absorbing risk that leveraged traders want to shed, and for being
willing to have your money tied up in a position that can be disrupted.

The funding rate is the market's price for that service. When it is high, it is high
because the service is in demand.
