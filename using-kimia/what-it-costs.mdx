title: "What it costs"
description: "The fees between the headline rate and what you keep."
---

The funding rate you see quoted is gross. Several things come out of it before the
money is yours.

## Costs you pay

**Exchange trading fees.** Both exchanges charge a fee on every order. You pay it four
times over the life of a position: buying and shorting on the way in, and closing both
on the way out.

**The spread.** Buying pushes the price up slightly and selling pushes it down. On
liquid assets this is negligible. On thin ones it is not, and it grows with your
order size.

**Network fees.** Solana transactions cost a small amount to submit. Hyperliquid
handles fees differently on its own chain.

**Dust.** The small remainder left behind when you close. See
[Closing a position](/using-kimia/closing).

None of these scale with how long you hold. They are paid at the start and the end.

## Why holding period matters more than rate

Because the costs are fixed per position and the yield accrues per hour, the same
position can be profitable or unprofitable depending only on how long you keep it.

A rate that looks excellent will not cover the round trip if you close after two
days. A modest rate held for two months usually will.

This is the single most common way to lose money on a strategy that is otherwise
working: opening and closing too often.

## Does Kimia charge a fee

The app does not currently add a fee of its own on top of the exchange costs above.
If that changes, it will be stated here and shown before you confirm an order.

Always read the confirmation screen. It is the authoritative statement of what a
given order will cost you.

## Working out whether it is worth it

Before opening, ask roughly: how many days of funding does it take to pay back the
round trip cost of getting in and out. If the answer is longer than you intend to
hold, do not open the position.

<Note>
Any annualized figure assumes a rate that holds for a year. Funding rates rarely hold
for a month. Treat quoted rates as a description of the present, not a projection.
</Note>
