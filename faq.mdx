---

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="Where does the yield actually come from">
A fee called the funding rate, paid hourly by leveraged traders on a perpetual futures
exchange. It is not newly issued tokens, not a share of protocol revenue, and not an
incentive programme. Somebody is genuinely paying it. See
[How it works](/how-it-works).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is the return guaranteed">
No. The funding rate changes hourly and can be negative, meaning the position costs
you money rather than paying you. Any annualized figure describes the present moment,
not the future.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can Kimia take my money">
No. Your funds sit in your own exchange account. The permission you grant covers
trading only, and the exchange enforces that limit. It cannot withdraw or transfer.
See [Your money](/safety/your-money).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why did I only sign once">
That signature approves an agent address to trade for you. Without it, every order
would need you present and signing. It is a trading permission, not a withdrawal
permission.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I lose money">
Yes. Funding can turn negative, the leveraged short side can be liquidated, an
exchange can auto deleverage your position, fees can exceed what you earned, and an
exchange itself can fail. [Risks](/safety/risks) covers each in detail.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What is the minimum">
Each side of a position must be worth at least 10 USD, making the practical floor
around 25 to 30 USD. That is the technical minimum. Fees are roughly fixed per
transaction, so small positions lose a large share of their yield to costs.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How long should I hold">
Long enough for the funding collected to exceed the cost of opening and closing.
Weeks rather than days. Frequent trading is the most common way people lose money on
this strategy.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why do I need to fund two exchanges separately">
Hyperliquid and Pacifica run on different blockchains and nothing bridges between
them. Money in one cannot open a position in the other. If you use only one exchange,
you only fund one.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="One side of my position shows a big loss">
Look at both together. If the long side shows a matching gain, the hedge is doing its
job. Neither number means anything on its own.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens if Kimia goes offline">
Your position stays open at the exchange and keeps accruing funding. You can close it
through the exchange's own interface at any time, without Kimia.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why was my order refused">
Kimia checks orders before placing them and refuses rather than proceeding when
something is outside its limits. A refusal is the system working.
[Troubleshooting](/safety/troubleshooting) lists the common reasons.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is there a token or an airdrop">
These docs cover using the product. Nothing here should be read as implying a
distribution of any kind.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do I stop">
Close your positions, then revoke the agent through each exchange you approved.
Withdraw from the exchange as normal. None of these steps require Kimia to cooperate.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>
